Question: Should an internally queued durable wake remain deliverable after the canonical session key has been reset to a new session generation, or must it be fenced to the generation that owned the wake?

Rationale: Both behaviors are technically implementable, but they encode different user-visible session ownership and stale-context guarantees; the existing queue deliberately treats `agentTurn` and `systemEvent` continuations differently, so code inspection alone cannot determine the intended durable-wake policy.

Likely owner: fuller-stack-dev

Owner confidence: high

Options:

- **Fence to source generation (recommended):** Record the expected session id on the durable wake queue entry and skip or supersede it after a reset, preventing stale wake context from entering a new conversation.
- **Survive session resets:** Keep the session-key-scoped `systemEvent` behavior, but document it as an intentional durable ownership transfer and add live proof that the post-reset experience is safe and understandable.
- **Defer the handoff:** Keep wake inspection and persisted attempts but postpone automatic queue handoff until the reset and ownership contract is settled.
