## Summary

Keep open: current main and v2026.6.11 still lack a system-wide cron tool policy such as `tools.cron.tools.allow/deny`, and the remaining decision is a security-sensitive config/default choice rather than a cleanup close.

## What This Changes

Request a cron-scoped tool restriction config, or explicit cron inheritance from subagent tool policy, so scheduled isolated jobs can be denied tools such as `message` system-wide.

## Best Possible Solution

Approve and implement a cron-scoped tool-policy contract with explicit precedence, schema/types/help/label/docs updates, and regression coverage for isolated cron message-tool denial across delivery modes.

- **Add Cron-Scoped Policy (recommended):** Introduce `tools.cron.tools.allow/deny` with documented precedence and tests for message-tool denial across cron delivery modes.
- **Inherit Subagent Policy:** Apply `tools.subagents.tools` to cron sessions too, reducing config surface while coupling two different execution modes.
- **Keep Per-Job Only:** Avoid a new global cron policy and rely on `payload.toolsAllow`, docs, and delivery safeguards for scheduled runs.

## Reproduction Assessment

Yes, at source level: current schema/types lack `tools.cron.tools`, and current policy resolution only applies subagent policy to subagent envelopes while cron relies on per-job `toolsAllow`.

## Solution Assessment

Unclear until maintainers choose the security and product direction. A cron-scoped policy appears cleaner than overloading subagent semantics, but the best fix must define precedence and upgrade behavior first.
