## Summary

Keep open: current main and v2026.6.11 still lack a cron-scoped `tools.cron.tools.allow/deny` policy or inheritance from `tools.subagents.tools`; the remaining work is a security-sensitive config/product decision rather than conservative cleanup.

## What This Changes

Request a system-wide cron job tool-restriction config, such as `tools.cron.tools.deny`, or explicit inheritance from `tools.subagents.tools`, so isolated cron runs cannot use disallowed tools like `message`.

## Best Possible Solution

Approve and implement a cron-scoped tool-policy contract with explicit precedence, schema/types/docs updates, and regression coverage for isolated cron message-tool denial across delivery modes.

Question: Should isolated cron jobs get a first-class `tools.cron.tools.allow/deny` policy, inherit `tools.subagents.tools`, or continue with only per-job `payload.toolsAllow` plus delivery safeguards?

Rationale: This defines a scheduled-run tool-access boundary and public config contract, including whether existing announce-mode cron jobs that directly use `message` keep working by default.

Likely owner: steipete

Owner reason: steipete has the strongest combined history on the scoped tool-policy and cron config decision surface.

Owner confidence: high

Options:

- **Add Cron-Scoped Policy (recommended):** Introduce `tools.cron.tools.allow/deny` with explicit precedence and tests for message-tool denial across cron delivery modes.
- **Inherit Subagent Policy:** Apply `tools.subagents.tools` to cron sessions too, reducing config surface but coupling two different execution modes.
- **Keep Per-Job Only:** Avoid a new global cron policy and rely on `payload.toolsAllow`, docs, and delivery safeguards for scheduled runs.

## Reproduction Assessment

Yes, at source level. Current schemas and types still lack `tools.cron.tools`, and current policy resolution only applies the subagent policy to subagent envelopes while cron relies on per-job `toolsAllow` and delivery safeguards.

## Solution Assessment

Unclear until maintainers choose the security and product direction. A cron-scoped policy appears cleaner than overloading subagent semantics, but the best fix must define precedence and upgrade behavior first.
