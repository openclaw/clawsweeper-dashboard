## Summary

Keep open. Current main now provides public namespace-claim intake and guarded recovery operations, but it does not settle the RFC’s permanent evidence thresholds, disputed-ownership rules, deleted-owner treatment, or public recovery guarantees; the collaborator-authored, security-sensitive policy discussion therefore remains necessary.

## What This Changes

Define transparent policy for organization and owner-namespace claims, disputes, deleted-owner namespaces, and recovery after owner-initiated account deletion.

## Best Possible Solution

Adopt a durable security specification and matching public documentation that define non-sensitive claim evidence, private verification handoff, competing-claim principles, deleted-owner namespace disposition, self-deletion recovery eligibility, ban and abuse exclusions, restoration scope, audit requirements, and clear user-facing outcomes; then add only the focused admin or test follow-ups required by that accepted contract.

## Maintainer Decision

Required: true

Kind: security_boundary

Question: What permanent authorization and disclosure contract should ClawHub use for disputed namespaces and recovery after owner-initiated deletion?

Rationale: Source code can implement a chosen rule, but it cannot determine which identity evidence is sufficient, whether deletion forfeits namespace rights, what may be disclosed publicly, or which security and moderation states permanently block recovery.

Likely owner: Patrick-Erichsen

Owner reason: They authored both the RFC and the merged public namespace-claim intake work, giving them the strongest available continuity for coordinating the final policy decision.

Owner confidence: high

Options:

- **Define bounded recovery policy (recommended):** Publish a policy allowing verified claims and eligible self-deletion recovery through audited staff review while keeping bans, abuse actions, competing claims, and sensitive evidence on a separate private path.
- **Keep discretionary staff review:** Document only the intake channels and leave evidence thresholds, namespace disposition, and recovery scope to unpublished case-by-case staff judgment.
- **Make deletion final:** Declare owner-initiated deletion permanently non-recoverable and release or reserve associated namespaces under a separately documented fixed rule.

- An overly permissive recovery rule could let deleted or inactive identities reclaim publishers or namespaces despite bans, abuse findings, or competing verified claims.
